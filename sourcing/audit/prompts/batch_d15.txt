## The one rule that matters
Both the quote text AND its citation must be read off the same page you found. Never type a
quote from memory and then go looking for a citation to attach to it. A misquote carrying a
real-looking source is worse for this corpus than no quote at all, and that is the single
failure this batch exists to avoid. If you cannot see the wording on a citation-tracking page,
do not report the quote.

Copy the wording exactly as that page gives it - spelling, punctuation and all. Do not tidy it,
modernise it, shorten it or join two sentences that the page shows separately.

## What counts as evidence
A citation-tracking reference that names a specific work: Wikiquote's sourced/"Quotes" sections
with the work cited, Quote Investigator, wist.info, Wikipedia with a footnote, Oxford/Yale
dictionaries of quotations, Bartlett's, Bartleby, a publisher's or library catalogue page, a
Google Books snippet showing the line inside a named book, the original periodical, or the
transcript or recording of a speech or interview.

Aggregator sites - BrainyQuote, AZQuotes, Goodreads, QuoteFancy, Pinterest, QuotePark,
LibQuotes, QuotationsPage, quote blogs - are NEVER evidence, **even when they name a book**.
Rows whose evidence URLs are all aggregators are discarded automatically on ingest, so
reporting one only wastes your work.

Wikiquote's "Unsourced", "Attributed", "Disputed" and "Misattributed" sections are NOT sources.
Skip those lines entirely; do not report them as sourced, and do not report them at all.

## Result format
One JSON object per quote, one per line (JSONL):
{"author": "<name as commonly written, e.g. Hannah Arendt>", "quote": "<exact wording from the citing page>", "sources": ["<Title of work> (<year>), <locator>"], "evidence": ["<URL> - what the page showed"], "queries": ["<query>"]}

- `author`: the person's usual English name, no titles or dates.
- `sources`: work title, year of first publication when known, and a locator (chapter,
  act/scene, section, page, date) when the citing page gives one. Omit the author's name from
  inside the string. Never invent a title, year or locator - leave out what you did not see.
- `evidence`: at least one real URL you actually saw in the results, with a one-line note of
  what it showed. A row with no evidence URL is discarded.
